Literature Review.

A targeted survey of honeypot taxonomy, adaptive cyber deception, host and Active Directory deception, honeytokens, and MITRE ATT&CK / D3FEND mapping. The review's purpose is not encyclopaedic — it is to extract the gap that the project's architecture is designed to close.

§ 1.1 — Themes
§1.1Themes Reviewed

Eight strands of the literature, traced for what they imply about layered deception.

Each strand was read with one filter: does this give us a credible reason to extend an environment past the first sensor? Strands that only described detection were tagged but not weighted; strands describing post-compromise observability and behavioural evaluation were treated as load-bearing.

Honeypot & honeynet taxonomy Adaptive cyber deception Military deception principles Host & Active Directory deception Honeytokens & decoy data MITRE ATT&CK / D3FEND mapping Attacker decision-making Behavioural evaluation of deception
§ 1.2 — Findings
§1.2Established Findings

Three convergent observations across the surveyed work.

The reviewed literature does not always agree on technique, but it converges on three observations about where deception research currently stops. Those three observations are the load-bearing premise of this project's design.

Finding · 01

Single-layer blindness

Conventional low- and high-interaction honeypots capture scanning, brute-force, and initial exploitation well — but are structurally weak at observing lateral movement, credential harvesting, collection, and exfiltration.

Finding · 02

Environmental continuity

Multi-stage observation requires plausible continuation paths. Attackers must encounter a believable progression from exposed services to internal hosts to high-value data or cloud resources.

Finding · 03

Behavioural evaluation

The field is shifting from detection-only metrics toward behavioural measures — misdirection, delay, engagement depth, path expansion, tactic-level observation — that better describe what deception actually changes.

§ 1.3 — Gap
§1.3Core Literature Gap

Existing work names the gap; few studies close it under controlled conditions.

Across the surveyed corpus, deception techniques, adaptive honeynets, and defensive frameworks are well-discussed in isolation. What remains underdeveloped is controlled, integrated evaluation of how layered deception changes attacker navigation across network, host, and data/cloud stages.

How does layered deception measurably change attacker behaviour, and what evidence — beyond alert volume — should defenders look at to know?
Project's response to the gap
Design an integrated three-layer deception environment that makes attacker paths observable, comparable, and measurable. The architectural and measurement work in Part II is the operationalisation of this response.
§ 1.4 — Matrix
§1.4Literature → Design · Evidence Matrix

Every architectural commitment traces back to a row in this matrix.

The matrix is the load-bearing seam between literature and architecture. If a row's finding turns out to be wrong, the corresponding architectural commitment is wrong. This makes the design defensible on first principles rather than on novelty alone.

Literature Area Key Finding Design Implication
Traditional honeypots Capture scanning, brute-force, and initial exploitation; rarely sustain post-compromise behaviour because the environment ends at the sensor edge. Architecture cannot be a single exposed surface — believable follow-on systems must sit behind it.
Adaptive deception Deception is more convincing when the environment responds to observed actions rather than exposing every lure simultaneously. Adopt progressive revelation: deeper layers become visible only after meaningful interaction with shallower ones.
Host / AD deception Internal identity and credentials are central to modern lateral movement decisions — attackers reason against the directory, not the network. Layer 2 includes a real Active Directory (GOAD), decoy accounts, planted credentials, and honeyfiles — not just additional Linux hosts.
Honeytokens & data lures Tokens and decoy files are most informative when uniquely attributable and tied to plausible attacker goals. Cloud keys, honeyfiles, decoy storage are tagged with artifact IDs so usage reconstructs attacker intent.
MITRE ATT&CK / D3FEND Standard frameworks supply a vocabulary connecting observed actions to tactics and defensive deception techniques. All telemetry is tagged with tactic codes; metric framework is reported per ATT&CK family.
Behavioural evaluation Detection-rate alone cannot demonstrate behaviour change; the field is shifting toward path-level metrics. Measurement framework is built around misdirection, delay, engagement rather than alert volume.
§ 1.5 — Questions
§1.5Research Questions Crystallised

Three falsifiable questions, each tied to one observable.

The literature work converges into three sub-questions, each terminating in an observable that the architecture (Part II) is engineered to surface. Each question is falsifiable on its own.

i.
RQ 1 — Navigation

How does layered deception influence attacker navigation across the environment?

Whether attackers follow paths created by deceptive services, planted credentials, decoy hosts, honeyfiles, and cloud references — and whether those paths look meaningfully different from a flat sensor's exhaust. Observable: per-session path traces.

ii.
RQ 2 — Decision

Which deception artifacts measurably affect attacker choices?

The design treats credentials, honeyfiles, fake accounts, cloud keys, and service banners as probes. Their differential pickup rate signals what attackers believed valuable, reachable, or trustworthy. Observable: artifact pickup rates by category.

iii.
RQ 3 — Engagement

Can staged layers increase meaningful engagement beyond initial access?

Whether progressive exposure encourages attackers to move from network reconnaissance into host enumeration, credential harvesting, and data/cloud-oriented behaviour. Observable: layer-progression depth + tactic diversity.

Layered deception should expose richer attacker behaviour than isolated honeypots —
and that richness must be measurable as path expansion, dwell, and tactic coverage.

Part II Completed

Architecture Design.

A three-layer honeynet — network, host/AD, data/cloud — built around four design principles, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, instrumented through a normalised event schema, and evaluated through a session-based measurement framework that operationalises misdirection, delay, and engagement.

✓ Four design principles ✓ Three layers specified ✓ Realism/lure calibration ✓ ATT&CK coverage matrix ✓ Anti-patterns avoided ✓ Event schema defined ✓ Measurement framework
§ 2.1 — Principles
§2.1Design Principles

Four commitments that hold across every layer.

These four principles are not aspirational — they are filters. Any proposed component, lure, credential, or service must be defensible against all four before being introduced into the environment.

P · 01

Plausibility over Perfection

The environment is judged by whether attackers continue, not by whether it is indistinguishable from a real enterprise. Believability is sufficient; perfection is wasteful.

P · 02

Progressive Friction

Each layer surfaces only after demonstrated interest in the previous one. Total exposure flattens engagement and tells attackers the environment is a sensor, not a system.

P · 03

Observable by Default

Any artifact that cannot be logged with a unique identifier does not enter the environment. Unobservable deception is not defensible.

P · 04

Safe Containment

No real credentials, no real production trust, no live outbound exfiltration paths. Realism is bounded by an explicit ethical and operational boundary.

§ 2.2 — Architecture
§2.2Layered Architecture

Three layers, each tuned to one phase of the attack lifecycle.

Layers are not isolated honeypots clustered together — they form a continuation pipeline, with planted credentials and references threading the attacker from one layer to the next.

§ 2.3 — Calibration
§2.3Realism vs. Lure Calibration

Where to sit on the realism–lure axis, by dimension.

A honeynet that is too realistic offers no signal (attackers discover nothing); one that is too obvious is dismissed (attackers disengage). The project explicitly chooses a position on each of four design dimensions.

Dimension Higher Realism Higher Lure Chosen Balance
Vulnerabilities subtle, chained, defensible obvious, isolated, single-step moderate · chained
Credentials hard to find, scoped tightly easy, abundant, global discoverable but scoped
Progression slow, logical, intra-system fast, noisy, cross-domain guided but misleading
Cost & Stability high · high-fidelity replicas low · throwaway scripts academic-feasible
Calibration is policy, not setting
The four positions above are environment-wide defaults. Individual assets may sit elsewhere — e.g. a single fake admin account placed deliberately easy-to-find functions as a high-lure tripwire. The calibration table is the policy, not a uniform setting.

§ 2.5 — Anti-patterns
§2.5Anti-Patterns Avoided

Six failure modes deliberately designed against.

Most failed honeynets fail in predictable ways. Listing the failures explicitly disciplines the build: each anti-pattern below was rejected with a stated reason, and tested for in the design review of every component.

✗ · 01

Overtly fake assets

Hostnames like test123 or admin_fake; empty files; placeholder content; perfectly uniform configurations.

Skilled attackers disengage early; engagement metrics collapse and the layered claim becomes unprovable.
✗ · 02

Single-step total compromise

One vulnerability that grants immediate domain-admin-equivalent access; flat administrative trust; no friction between layers.

No measurable misdirection or delay; Layer 2 becomes meaningless and the architecture collapses to a single sensor.
✗ · 03

Over-hardened systems

Patched-to-the-edge services; strong, unique credentials everywhere; nothing actionable for an attacker to do after first contact.

No attacker progression; the project degrades into a passive listener with nothing to listen for.
✗ · 04

Unobservable deception

Missing authentication logs; no process or command telemetry; honeyfiles whose access cannot be uniquely attributed.

Actions cannot be mapped to ATT&CK; data is unusable for the thesis; the design loses its falsifiability.
✗ · 05

Real external trust or credentials

Real cloud accounts; real API keys; live outbound exfiltration channels; any genuine production data left inside the lab.

Ethical and institutional risk; violates the academic safety boundary that lets the work be defended at all.
✗ · 06

Overly complex services

Full enterprise applications stood up purely for atmosphere; dependencies that double the operational surface without doubling the telemetry surface.

Setup time inflates, instability rises, and infrastructure debugging crowds out the research questions.
§ 2.6 — Observability
§2.6Observability & Event Schema

Three primitives turn raw logs into reconstructable behaviour.

Cross-layer behaviour is recoverable only when three commitments hold: every deceptive object has an identity; every event uses a normalised schema; every action carries the artifact ID it touched. The combination is what permits session reconstruction across heterogeneous sensors.

Primitive What it provides Why it matters for the thesis
Artifact tagging Each decoy service, credential, file, account, and cloud key receives a unique artifact_id. Lets later interactions be traced back to the lure that caused them — the precondition for measuring decoy-induced behaviour.
Path reconstruction Events are correlated into attacker sessions across services, hosts, and cloud surfaces. Without it, layered evidence collapses into per-sensor noise; with it, RQ1 (navigation) becomes answerable.
Behavioural metrics Misdirection, delay, and engagement (see §2.7) translate raw telemetry into evidence. Promotes the architecture from "alert generator" to "behavioural instrument."

§ 2.7 — Measurement
§2.7Measurement Framework

Three metric families, each session-derived, each defensible.

Because the environment is composed entirely of deceptive assets, classical "real vs. fake" misdirection metrics do not apply. The framework instead operationalises misdirection as path deviation and decoy interaction, delay as dwell time, and engagement as depth and tactical diversity.

MisdirectionM1 · M2 · M3

Definition

Degree to which attackers follow decoy-induced paths or interact with explicitly deceptive elements rather than minimal-effort paths.

Operationalisation

DPER = Decoy-Element Actions / Total Actions PEF = Observed Action Count / Minimal-Path Action Count DIC = count( actions on dead-end assets )

Decoy elements include

Fake AD users · honey API keys · decoy S3 buckets · non-functional admin shares · attributed honeyfiles.

DelayD1 · D2 · D3

Definition

Additional time attackers spend interacting with deceptive assets before disengaging or progressing.

Operationalisation

Session Duration = last_log − first_log Layer Dwell = Σ time-in-layer per layer Time-to-Abandonment = idle window after last action

Why these three

Total dwell quantifies overall friction; per-layer dwell isolates where deception worked; abandonment surfaces the inflection point of disengagement.

EngagementE1 · E2 · E3

Definition

Depth and diversity of attacker actions across the environment.

Operationalisation

Action Count = count( logged events ) Distinct Techniques = | { unique attck_technique } | Layer Depth = max( layer_reached ) ∈ {1,2,3}

Aggregated across sessions to compare

Layered vs. flat exposure · staged-activation policies · lure rotation strategies.

Methodology rationale
Because the HoneyNet contains only deceptive assets, traditional "real vs. fake" misdirection comparisons do not apply. Misdirection is therefore operationalised as path deviation and decoy interaction — reflecting the behavioural impact of layering rather than the existence of decoys. Delay and engagement come from observable timestamps, action counts, and reached depth. The result is replicable, quantifiable, and academically defensible.

§ 3.6 — Open issues
§3.6Outstanding Considerations

Live tradeoffs the next phase must resolve.

Some design questions are deliberately unresolved at this stage; resolving them prematurely would foreclose options the experimental phase needs to explore. The four open considerations below are surfaced explicitly so review can sharpen them.

▸ Open · 01

Realism vs. safety on the boundary

How realistic can the environment appear without weakening the safety boundary? Network segmentation, containment controls, service exposure, and outbound restriction interact in ways that can collapse one constraint while improving another. Active design consideration.

▸ Open · 02

Logging completeness vs. operational noise

Per-action telemetry is a hard requirement, but pushing every event through the normalisation pipeline carries cost. The project must decide where in the pipeline to filter, sample, or summarise without losing the artifact-ID joins that make session reconstruction possible.

▸ Open · 03

Research depth vs. implementation breadth

The project carries both a research and an engineering burden. Balancing literature work, architectural design, infrastructure deployment, and documentation requires explicit prioritisation as the system grows.

▸ Open · 04

Staged-activation policy tuning

The architecture commits to progressive revelation but the activation policy itself — what attacker signal triggers L2 exposure, what triggers L3 — is a tuneable surface that the experimental phase will explore as an independent variable.
